"Nearshore" means hiring a software team in a nearby country instead of across the world. For US companies, Mexico is the most natural option: same continent, overlapping hours and a short flight. But the word gets used for everything from a single freelancer to a 500-person outsourcing firm.

This guide covers what actually changes when a US company works with a team in Mexico, and the questions that separate a reliable partner from a risky one. We are a founder-led studio in Puebla, Mexico, so we will use our own setup as a concrete example.

## Time zones: how much do the hours overlap?

Most of central Mexico, including Puebla and Mexico City, stays on UTC−6 all year. Mexico stopped observing daylight saving time in most of the country in 2022. In practice:

| Your office | Winter | Summer |
|---|---|---|
| Chicago (Central) | Same time | Mexico is 1 hour behind |
| New York (Eastern) | Mexico is 1 hour behind | Mexico is 2 hours behind |
| Los Angeles (Pacific) | Mexico is 2 hours ahead | Mexico is 1 hour ahead |

That means standups, design reviews and same-day bug fixes happen during your normal working day, not at midnight.

## Language and communication

Ask who you will actually talk to. In large firms, a sales lead and an account manager often sit between you and the engineers. In a founder-led studio, the person writing or reviewing the code is usually on the call.

Good signs:

- You talk to the technical lead in English, without a translator.
- Written updates are short and concrete: what shipped, what is blocked, what is next.
- You get builds you can install and test, not just status reports.

## Contracts and intellectual property

This is where many first-time nearshore projects go wrong. Your agreement should state, in writing:

- **Full assignment of IP** for the code, designs and documentation to your company.
- **Repositories under your account** (GitHub, GitLab, Bitbucket) from day one, or at least at every milestone.
- **App Store, Google Play, cloud and domain accounts in your company's name.**
- **An NDA** signed before you share business details.
- **Which law governs the contract** and how disputes are resolved.

Have your own lawyer review the contract. A reliable partner will not push back on any of the points above.

## Payments, invoices and tax forms

- **Currency:** contracts and invoices can be in US dollars, paid by wire or card. Ask about fees on both sides.
- **Tax forms:** a foreign contractor typically gives you a **W-8BEN** (individuals) or **W-8BEN-E** (entities) instead of a W-9. Your accountant will tell you what you need.
- **Mexican invoices (CFDI):** if your company also has a Mexican entity, your vendor can issue a CFDI, the electronic invoice required by Mexico's tax authority.

## Engagement models

| Model | Best for | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| Fixed-scope project | A defined MVP or feature set | Change requests billed without prior agreement |
| Dedicated team (monthly) | A product that evolves every week | Paying for hours without visible output |
| Maintenance retainer | An app already in production | Response times that aren't written into the contract |

## How to vet a nearshore partner

1. **Ask for software in production.** Store listings and live systems show update dates, ratings and reviews that nobody can fake.
2. **Talk to the person who will lead your project**, not only to sales.
3. **Ask how they handle the first two weeks.** You should see a plan, screens and a first build quickly.
4. **Check what happens after launch.** Apple and Google raise their requirements every year; an app nobody maintains stops working well on new phones.
5. **Start small.** A short paid discovery or a first milestone tells you more than any sales deck.
